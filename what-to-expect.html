What to expect during a stump grinding project

Knowing the general sequence helps you prepare your yard and avoid surprises. A typical grinding visit follows these steps.

1. Assessment and quote

A provider looks at each stump's diameter, the surrounding roots, access for the machine, and any obstacles. They confirm what "done" means for you — grind to grade, deeper, chips left or hauled — and give a price before work begins.

2. Utility and irrigation locating

Before anything grinds below grade, buried utilities should be marked. In most of the U.S. that starts with a free 811 "call before you dig" locate request. Also flag private lines a public locate won't cover: irrigation, low-voltage lighting, invisible pet fences, and septic components.

3. Site prep

The area around the stump is cleared of rocks, debris, and loose objects that could be thrown by the cutting wheel or damage it. Pets and children should be kept well away during grinding.

4. The grind

The machine's wheel works across the stump in passes, lowering it below grade. Surface roots can be ground too if you requested it. This is the fast part — often well under an hour for a typical single stump, longer for large or multiple stumps.

5. Cleanup

Grinding leaves a mound of wood chips mixed with soil. Options: spread them as mulch, keep a pile, or have them hauled away. If you plan to replant grass, the hollow is usually topped with soil so it can be seeded.

6. After the job

Remaining roots decay over months to years. If you are replanting a tree in the same spot, or building on it, tell the provider up front — that may call for deeper grinding or full removal instead. Fresh chips can temporarily tie up nitrogen, so many people remove chips before seeding.

How to prepare

  • Mark or describe private irrigation, lighting, and pet-fence lines.
  • Clear vehicles and furniture from the machine's path.
  • Decide in advance what you want done with the chips.
  • Confirm the grinding depth that matches your plans for the space.
